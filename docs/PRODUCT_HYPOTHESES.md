# Product hypotheses and validation needs

| Hypothesis | Current evidence | What would strengthen it | What could disconfirm it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Some aspirants struggle to apply familiar concepts to unfamiliar question framings. | Retrospective mentoring observations and anecdotal public discussions. | Real-user attempts showing a repeated transfer problem and improvement on unseen items. | Students solve transfer items without the proposed flow, or their main issue is a prerequisite knowledge gap. |
| Progressive hints can support reasoning without giving away the answer. | Product design rationale only. | Participants use early hints and still solve a subsequent unseen item independently. | Most learners require the final hint or simply copy the supplied structure. |
| Mixed-subject micro-practice is useful near the exam. | Small survey responses and one prototype session; not representative. | Repeat use, completion and positive observed task outcomes in real aspirants. | Students prefer focused practice or abandon the mixed items. |
| Personalised feedback is valued by potential users. | In a small self-selected survey, 13/14 valid responses selected personalised feedback as a reason to try a free platform. | Larger, more varied sample plus observed use and return behaviour. | Stated interest does not turn into engagement or meaningful outcomes. |
| A local rule-based coach is useful as an MVP support. | A basic implementation exists, but no efficacy evidence. | Students use it selectively and can solve fresh items afterwards. | The coach gives away key reasoning or is no more useful than static explanations. |
| Explicit difficulty choices and varied task types improve perceived fit. | v9 contains Low / Medium / High question banks plus Quant, VARC, scoring and visual DI tasks. | Real users select a level that matches their current need and complete the tasks with appropriate challenge. | The levels feel miscalibrated or different sections distract from the product’s core learning hypothesis. |
| Rule-based session feedback helps students choose a next action. | v9 derives a focus area from errors and skips, but this is a design assumption. | Recommendations are judged relevant and help the user solve an unseen follow-up item. | Users disagree with the diagnosis or cannot apply the suggested action. |

## Constraints

These are hypotheses, not proven findings. Survey results are directional and should be reported with the actual denominators and limitations. Public online discussions are secondary evidence, not primary interviews. Retrospective mentoring recollections must be labelled separately from research interviews.
